Scenario 1: University Systems.

To avoid legal complications, the university must use a private cloud to store and access sensitive student data. This is why I would prefer utilizing private cloud because the University requires such reliance on this system for its infrastructure.

For the public course catalog website, I would go with a cloud system where the catalogue is viewable by everyone, and visitors register frequently. This alternative to the student records system eliminates the need for physical servers on campus, while also providing resources that can handle increased traffic. A public cloud solution?

To ensure optimal efficiency, the university should use a combination of cloud solutions, including utilizing private clouds for student records and public clouds to manage course catalogues, rather than consolidating them into unified systems. This approach emphasizes individual system requirements.

Scenario 2: PaaS vs IaaS.

The notion that PaaS is always cheaper than Iaas is incorrect. While PaaS can reduce infrastructure costs by ensuring that the provider handles more of the underlying environment, it may not be the most cost-effective option.

If a company uses specialized server configurations for routine workloads that can be easily predicted, IaaS could be viewed as reducing costs and simplifying technical setups. However, with PaaS, the company may pay for additional managed features and platform resources that it does not require.

The cost-effectiveness I believe is based on workload, resources, level of authority, and the extent of infrastructure setup and management that your business is willing to undertake.
